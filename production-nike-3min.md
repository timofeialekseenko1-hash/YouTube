## NARRATION (one line per block, ~8–9 s of speech each)

**Block 1** — In nineteen sixty-four, a twenty-four-year-old accountant drove around Oregon selling Japanese running shoes out of the trunk of his green Plymouth Valiant.
**Block 2** — He had twelve hundred dollars, no store, and a bet everyone called insane. Today that bet is worth one hundred fifty billion dollars.
**Block 3** — His name was Phil Knight, a middle-distance runner who was never quite fast enough, trained by a coach obsessed with shaving off seconds.
**Block 4** — At Stanford, Knight wrote a paper arguing cheap, high-quality Japanese shoes could crush the German brands dominating athletics. It was just a class assignment.
**Block 5** — In Japan, he walked into the Onitsuka factory claiming to represent an American distributor. That company did not exist. He invented it on the spot.
**Block 6** — The first three hundred pairs arrived in nineteen sixty-four. Knight kept his day job and sold shoes at track meets, runner by runner.
**Block 7** — For seven years cash was brutally tight. Banks rejected him again and again, and every dollar of profit went straight back into more shoes.
**Block 8** — Then, in nineteen seventy-one, betrayal. His Japanese supplier was quietly negotiating with another distributor, preparing to cut him out completely.
**Block 9** — Knight refused to wait for the end. He walked away first, gambling everything on building his own shoe under his own name.
**Block 10** — A design student was paid thirty-five dollars for a logo. Knight said he didn't love it, but it would grow on him. They called it Nike.
**Block 11** — Then came the strangest breakthrough in business history. His old coach stared at a waffle iron, and poured liquid rubber straight into it.
**Block 12** — The iron was ruined, but the waffle sole gripped like nothing else. By nineteen seventy-four it was America's best-selling training shoe.
**Block 13** — The jogging boom hit, and Nike exploded from ten million dollars in sales to nearly two hundred seventy million in just four years.
**Block 14** — In nineteen eighty-four, Nike bet half a million dollars a year on an unproven rookie who had never played a single NBA game.
**Block 15** — His name was Michael Jordan. Analysts projected three million in sales. The Air Jordan made one hundred twenty-six million dollars in its first year.
**Block 16** — Just Do It became more than a slogan. Nike stopped selling shoes, and started selling belief, ambition, and identity itself.
**Block 17** — Today Nike earns over fifty billion dollars a year, and that old green Plymouth still sits in the lobby of its headquarters.
**Block 18** — A car trunk, twelve hundred dollars, and a supplier who betrayed them. That is how empires actually begin. Subscribe for the next story.
