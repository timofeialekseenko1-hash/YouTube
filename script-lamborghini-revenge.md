# PRODUCTION SCRIPT — "Ferrari Laughed at This Tractor Maker. It Cost Them Everything."
**Format:** 21 blocks × 10 s = 3:30 · 16:9 · Mixed Media collage · mascot narrator ("The Storyteller")
**Narration style (NEW — Zenn-influenced):** continuous essay flow. Every line is written to land **9.5–10.5 seconds** of speech (~27–30 words) so the assembler never pads silence — slight overruns are sped up pitch-safely, which makes blocks butt seamlessly. Sentences deliberately bridge across scene changes.
**Tone:** dry humor woven in, second-person address in the final act, 20-second viewer encouragement ending, signature channel sign-off.

**Title options (Zenn-style curiosity gap > brand formula):**
1. "Ferrari Laughed at This Tractor Maker. It Cost Them Everything." ← recommended
2. "The $2 Million Revenge: Why Lamborghini Exists"
3. "He Was Told to Stick to Tractors. So He Built Lamborghini."

## NARRATION (one line per 10s block, ~27–30 words each, flows continuously)

**Block 1** *(HERO ANIMATED SCENE 1 — cold open)*
In nineteen sixty-three, one of the richest men in Italy was told, to his face, that he should shut up and go back to his tractors. He didn't take it well.

**Block 2**
Instead of writing an angry letter like a normal person, he spent the modern equivalent of millions building a machine designed to humiliate the man who insulted him.

**Block 3**
That man was Ferruccio Lamborghini, and to understand why his revenge worked, you need to know that he was never supposed to be rich in the first place.

**Block 4**
He was born in nineteen sixteen to grape farmers, obsessed with engines instead of vineyards, and during World War Two the Italian air force made him a mechanic on an isolated island.

**Block 5**
When the war ended, Italy was broke and littered with abandoned military machines, so Ferruccio did something beautifully practical: he dragged them home and turned old war engines into farm tractors.

**Block 6**
Farmers needed cheap tractors, Ferruccio had a warehouse of leftover war, and by the nineteen fifties his tractor company had made him one of the wealthiest industrialists in the entire country.

**Block 7**
And like every wealthy Italian man of that era, he celebrated the only reasonable way: by collecting sports cars. Maseratis, Mercedes, Jaguars — and above all, Ferraris.

**Block 8**
There was just one problem. His Ferraris kept breaking. Specifically the clutch — the same part, again and again, and every repair meant weeks of waiting and eye-watering bills from Maranello.

**Block 9** *(HERO ANIMATED SCENE 2 — the insult)*
So in nineteen sixty-three, Ferruccio drove to Ferrari headquarters and told Enzo Ferrari himself that his beautiful cars had a clutch problem. Enzo's reply became automotive legend.

**Block 10**
"You're a tractor maker. You don't know anything about sports cars." Now, most of us would have just bought a Maserati and moved on. Ferruccio was not most of us.

**Block 11**
Back at his factory, he opened up his broken Ferrari clutch and discovered something glorious: it was the exact same clutch he installed in his tractors — marked up roughly ten times.

**Block 12**
He'd been paying supercar prices for tractor parts. That was the moment the anger became a business plan: if Ferrari was built from parts like these, he could do better himself.

**Block 13**
He poached brilliant young engineers, some straight from Ferrari's own orbit, gave them one instruction — build the grand touring car I couldn't buy — and gave them almost no time to do it.

**Block 14**
Four months later, at the Turin Motor Show, the first Lamborghini prototype rolled out, and for its badge Ferruccio chose his own zodiac sign: the raging bull, aimed directly at Ferrari.

**Block 15** *(HERO ANIMATED SCENE 3 — the Miura moment)*
Then in nineteen sixty-six his team did something nobody asked for: they put the engine behind the driver and created the Miura — widely called the first supercar ever made.

**Block 16**
Ferrari, the racing giant, was suddenly chasing a tractor company's design philosophy. And Ferruccio never even entered racing — he said he had nothing to prove on a track.

**Block 17**
He built cars the way he built tractors: for the customer, not for trophies. Sixty years later that insult still echoes — every Lamborghini ever sold is Enzo's mistake, multiplied.

**Block 18**
Today Lamborghinis sell for anywhere between a quarter million and over two million dollars, and the company that was told to stick to tractors is one of the most desired brands alive.

**Block 19**
And somewhere in that story is the best part: Ferruccio never stopped making tractors. He just also happened to make the fastest insult response in human history.

**Block 20** *(ENCOURAGEMENT — direct address, part 1)*
So here's the thing about you. Somewhere, someone has already decided what you are — your job title, your background, your tractor. They've filed you away, and honestly, that's their loss.

**Block 21** *(ENCOURAGEMENT + SIGN-OFF, part 2)*
The most expensive gift Enzo Ferrari ever gave away was one free insult. When you get yours, don't argue — build. And when it's built, come tell us the story. This channel runs on them.

## WHY THE ENDING WORKS (Zenn mechanic)
Blocks 20–21 = exactly 20 seconds. The story pivots from history to the viewer's own life ("your tractor"), lands one actionable emotional beat ("don't argue — build"), then makes the channel itself part of the loop ("come tell us the story. This channel runs on them") — that last line is the memorability hook and doubles as a comment-section prompt, which feeds the algorithm.
