## NARRATION + ON-SCREEN TEXT (one line per 10s block, ~24–27 words, speech_rate 20)

**B1** `SAME BRAIN` — Put a rich person and a poor person in the same brain scanner, and you will see a real, measurable difference. It is not intelligence. And it is not willpower.
**B2** `IT'S A TRAP` — It is a difference so powerful it can trap someone in poverty for life, or hand someone an invisible advantage they never earned. And you will spot it in yourself.
**B3** `INSTALLED` — Because the rich brain and the poor brain are not things you are born with. They are states your environment installs. Here is what is actually happening inside.
**B4** `THE STUDY` — Start with a landmark experiment. Scientists tested Indian farmers twice: once before harvest, when they were broke, and once after, when they had just been paid.
**B5** `MINUS 13 IQ` — Same farmers. Same brains. Yet while broke and stressed, they scored the equivalent of thirteen fewer I.Q. points than their own, richer, post-harvest selves.
**B6** `NOT ABILITY` — Thirteen points. That is the gap between average and gifted, erased not by ability, but by the pure mental weight of not having enough.
**B7** `BANDWIDTH TAX` — Psychologists call it the bandwidth tax. Scarcity hijacks your attention and runs in the background constantly, like a computer with far too many tabs open at once.
**B8** `NO POWER LEFT` — When part of your mind is always solving how do I make rent, there is less processing power for everything else. Plans. Patience. Careful decisions.
**B9** `THE LOOP` — This is the cruel part. Scarcity does not just mean having less. It actively makes you worse at the exact thinking that could help you escape it.
**B10** `CORTISOL` — Now add stress. Chronic money worry floods the brain with cortisol, and cortisol weakens the prefrontal cortex, your center for planning and self-control.
**B11** `ALARM ON` — At the same time, it supercharges the amygdala, your fear alarm. The survival brain gets louder. The strategic, long-term brain goes quiet.
**B12** `RATIONAL` — So grabbing a small reward now instead of a bigger one later is not foolish. It is rational, for a world where later has rarely felt safe.
**B13** `MARSHMALLOW LIE` — And this shatters a famous myth. The marshmallow test claimed patient kids succeed. But a newer study found the kids weren't more disciplined at all.
**B14** `THEY LEARNED` — The children who waited had simply learned that adults keep promises. The ones who grabbed it had learned the opposite: take it now, or lose it.
**B15** `IT'S TRUST` — Patience was never pure character. It is trust that the future will pay out, and scarcity quietly destroys that trust before a child can even speak.
**B16** `IT GETS DARKER` — Because it runs deeper than habits. It reaches into the developing brain itself. And this is where the research gets genuinely disturbing.
**B17** `SMALLER REGIONS` — Studies link growing up poor to measurable differences in children's brain structure, in the regions for language, memory, and self-control, with the largest gaps at the lowest incomes.
**B18** `NOT THEIR FAULT` — This is not destiny, and it is not their fault. It is chronic stress physically shaping a brain, before that child ever gets a say in it.
**B19** `PLOT TWIST` — But here is the twist nobody expects. Scarcity is not pure weakness. In the short term it makes people sharper, faster, more efficient at the problem in front of them.
**B20** `POOR = SHARPER` — People with less are often better budgeters under pressure, and measurably better at reading emotions, more tuned to others than the wealthy around them.
**B21** `THE RIGGED GAME` — Now flip to the rich brain. In one experiment, players were given a rigged game of Monopoly, secretly handed double the money and double the dice.
**B22** `THEY TOOK CREDIT` — The winners, who knew the game was rigged, still credited their victory to their own skill. Wealth quietly convinced them they had earned an advantage they were simply given.
**B23** `LEARNED HELPLESS` — There is a darker loop too. When effort keeps failing to pay off, the brain learns helplessness. It stops trying, which guarantees the very failure that taught it to stop.
**B24** `HIJACKED` — And scarcity hijacks reward itself. An overloaded, uncertain brain craves fast, guaranteed hits, the scroll, the snack, the lottery ticket, over slow, uncertain, long-term payoffs.
**B25** `SAME HARDWARE` — So let's kill the myth completely. Rich brains are not smarter, kinder, or more disciplined. They are, very often, simply less taxed. The hardware is identical.
**B26** `PLASTIC` — And that is the hopeful turn, backed by the same science. Brains are plastic. Change the inputs, and the wiring follows. Scarcity is a state, not a sentence.
**B27** `CLOSE A TAB` — You can start cutting the tax before the money arrives. Automating one bill closes a tab. A small emergency buffer literally quiets the fear alarm.
**B28** `OFFLOAD IT` — Writing decisions down instead of carrying them in your head hands power back to the prefrontal cortex. Every worry you offload is bandwidth returned to the long game.
**B29** `ENGINEER IT` — And the final secret? The most focused wealthy people guard their attention ruthlessly, deliberately starving themselves of distraction. They don't have more bandwidth. They protect it.
**B30** `MAKE ROOM` — So the real difference was never money. It is room to think. Build your life a little more of it, and your brain will follow. We will be right here, with the next story.

## FACT-CHECK FLAGS (all documented — verify before recording)
- Sugarcane / −13 IQ pts: Mani, Mullainathan, Shafir & Zhao, *Science* 2013.
- Bandwidth tax / tunneling: Mullainathan & Shafir, *Scarcity* (2013).
- Cortisol → PFC down / amygdala up under chronic stress: stress neuroscience (Arnsten). Say "tends to."
- Marshmallow reinterpretation: Kidd, Palmeri & Aslin, *Cognition* 2013 (reliability of environment drives waiting).
- Poverty & children's brain structure: Noble et al., *Nature Neuroscience* 2015 (family income ↔ cortical surface area; largest effect at low incomes). Correlational — frame carefully, "linked to," "not their fault."
- Scarcity focus dividend / better under-pressure budgeting & lower-class empathic accuracy: Mullainathan & Shafir; Kraus, Piff, Keltner (UC Berkeley).
- Rigged Monopoly study: Piff (UC Berkeley / TED). Advantaged players attributed wins to skill.
- Learned helplessness: Seligman — frame as theory/analogy.
- Dopamine/reward under scarcity & uncertainty: general reward neuroscience — keep loose.
- Neuroplasticity "state not a sentence": broadly true; keep tactics behavioral, not medical.
- Pinned comment: "General psychology, averages from studies, not individual advice or destiny."
