# PRODUCTION SCRIPT — "He Was Fired on a Friday. By Wednesday, He Was Unfireable."
**Format:** 24 blocks × 10 s = 4:00 · 16:9 · Mixed Media collage · mascot narrator
**Voice calibration (Lamborghini lesson applied):** Arthur reads comma-heavy lines at ~2.1–2.4 words/sec at default rate. All blocks below are written at 24–27 words and should be voiced with **speech_rate 20** baked in from the first take — no re-voicing round needed.
**Real-person policy:** Sam Altman appears ONLY as a stylized faceless paper silhouette ("the Man in the Gray Sweater" — recurring visual motif). No likeness, no fabricated quotes. Every direct quote in this script is documented public record. Speculation is always framed as a question, never a claim.

---

## HOOK ARCHITECTURE (user spec: 5s hook + 15-20s retention lock)

**Seconds 0–5 (first half of Block 1):** one sentence, maximum drama, no context — "the most powerful man in tech was fired by four people on a video call."
**Seconds 5–25 (rest of Block 1 + Block 2):** the retention lock is a *double open loop* — (1) five days later he was back and the people who fired him were gone; (2) the board knew something we still don't. Neither loop closes until minute 2, and loop 2 never fully closes — that's the comment bait.

---

## NARRATION (one line per block, ~24–27 words, speech_rate 20)

**Block 1** *(HERO SCENE 1 — cold open)*
In November twenty twenty-three, the most powerful man in tech was fired by four people on a video call. He ran the company building humanity's future.

**Block 2**
Five days later, he had his job back — and the people who fired him were gone. They said he wasn't honest with them. They never said about what.

**Block 3**
His name is Sam Altman, and whether you trust him may be the most expensive opinion you'll ever hold. So let's look at what we actually know.

**Block 4**
He was a Stanford dropout with a failed startup, who somehow became president of Y Combinator — the machine that built Airbnb, Dropbox, and Stripe — at twenty-eight.

**Block 5**
People who worked with him describe the same talent: he walks into a room wanting something, and walks out with everyone convinced it was their idea.

**Block 6**
Remember that talent. It's about to explain almost everything that happens in this story — including the parts that look impossible.

**Block 7**
In twenty fifteen he co-founded OpenAI as a nonprofit, alongside Elon Musk. The mission statement said artificial intelligence should benefit all of humanity. Not shareholders. Everyone.

**Block 8**
No profits, full transparency, safety first. If you wanted to design an organization the public could trust with the most dangerous technology ever built — it looked like this.

**Block 9**
Then the idealism met the electric bill. Training these systems costs billions, and nonprofits don't have billions, so in twenty nineteen the structure quietly changed.

**Block 10**
A for-profit arm appeared, Microsoft wired in its first billions, and the company called OpenAI began the process of becoming, well — increasingly closed.

**Block 11**
Then came ChatGPT. A hundred million users in two months — the fastest-adopted product in history — and suddenly Sam Altman was the face of the future.

**Block 12**
Congress asked him to testify. Presidents and prime ministers cleared their schedules. One man, elected by nobody, was now negotiating the terms of everyone's tomorrow.

**Block 13** *(HERO SCENE 2 — the firing)*
And then, on a Friday afternoon, his own board fired him. Their statement said he was, quote, not consistently candid in his communications with the board.

**Block 14**
Not consistently candid. Corporate language for something they refused to name. No scandal was cited, no crime, no smoking gun — just those words, and silence.

**Block 15**
What happened next had never happened in corporate history. Within seventy-two hours, more than seven hundred of OpenAI's seven hundred seventy employees threatened to walk out.

**Block 16**
Even the board member who led the firing publicly reversed himself and said he regretted it. By Tuesday night, Altman was back. The board was gone.

**Block 17**
Here's where it gets uncomfortable. One former board member later claimed the board learned ChatGPT had launched — from Twitter. They ran the company. Nobody told them.

**Block 18**
So one side reads this story as proof he's untouchable: fire him and the entire company follows him out the door. Loyalty like that is earned.

**Block 19**
The other side reads the exact same story as the problem: if a safety board cannot fire this man, then nobody on Earth can.

**Block 20**
And the people built to worry agreed. Within months, his chief scientist left, the safety team dissolved, and its leader said safety culture had taken a backseat to shiny products.

**Block 21**
The defense is real too: he took no equity for years, he asked Congress to regulate his own industry, and he shipped this technology to billions — free.

**Block 22**
So is he protecting humanity from the storm, or selling tickets to it? Here's the honest answer nobody on the internet will give you: we don't know yet.

**Block 23** *(direct address — the channel's signature turn)*
But here's what you can control. The most important skill of the next decade isn't using AI — it's watching the people who build it. Closely. Skeptically.

**Block 24** *(sign-off)*
Don't outsource your future to anyone's good intentions — audit them. We'll keep watching this story, and every story like it. That's what this channel is for.
