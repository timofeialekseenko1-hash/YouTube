## NARRATION (one line per 10s block, ~24–27 words, speech_rate 20)

**Block 1** *(HERO SCENE — cold open)*
In two thousand three, three Harvard students handed a nineteen-year-old their billion-dollar idea. Then they watched him allegedly take it, and build Facebook instead.

**Block 2**
What happened over the next ninety days became the most famous betrayal in tech history: a bitter lawsuit, a Hollywood movie, and a fortune. But the emails tell a darker story.

**Block 3**
Because this isn't really about who owned the idea. It's about a lesson every single one of you needs to hear. So let's rewind to a Harvard dorm room.

**Block 4**
Two thousand three. Three ambitious students, the Winklevoss twins, Olympic-level rowers, and their sharp partner Divya Narendra, believed they had spotted the future.

**Block 5**
They were building a social network just for Harvard students. Exclusive, elite, real names only. They called it HarvardConnection, and it could have been enormous.

**Block 6**
There was one problem. They had the vision, but they'd lost their programmer, and the site sat half-finished, stalled, going absolutely nowhere.

**Block 7**
So they hunted for the best coder on campus, a sophomore who'd just gone viral for a stunt that nearly got him expelled. His name was Mark Zuckerberg.

**Block 8**
Weeks earlier, Zuckerberg had built a site called Facemash, letting students rate their classmates' looks. It was so popular it crashed Harvard's network within hours.

**Block 9**
He was reckless, brilliant, and exactly the talent they needed. In November two thousand three, they made a deal. He would finish building their code.

**Block 10**
He said yes. They shook on it. No contract, no lawyer, just a handshake between students. And that handshake, they would later argue, cost them everything.

**Block 11**
At first, everything seemed fine. Zuckerberg told them the code was coming along nicely. He just needed a little more time to get it right.

**Block 12**
Then the excuses began. He was buried in schoolwork. He was fighting technical problems. His charger was broken. Meeting after meeting quietly slipped away.

**Block 13**
What the trio didn't know was that those emails and messages would one day be read aloud in a courtroom, revealing what he was allegedly really doing.

**Block 14**
Because while he told them he was stuck, Zuckerberg was allegedly pouring every free night into a completely different website. His own. Not theirs.

**Block 15**
In one private message, later made public, he reportedly mocked the very people who trusted him, joking that they had handed him everything, and trusted him completely.

**Block 16**
For roughly ninety days, the trio alleged, he stalled them, while quietly building the one thing that would erase their entire head start.

**Block 17** 
Then, on February fourth, two thousand four, a website went live. Not HarvardConnection. Not their project. It was called thefacebook dot com. And it was his.

**Block 18**
The trio had no warning. They found out the way everyone else did, watching it detonate across campus, the idea they had trusted him with, now carrying his name.

**Block 19**
They were furious. They took their case to Harvard's president and got nowhere. So they did the only thing they had left. They lawyered up, and they sued.

**Block 20**
The battle dragged on for years. Finally, in two thousand eight, it settled, reportedly for around sixty-five million dollars in cash and Facebook stock.

**Block 21**
But Zuckerberg always denied stealing anything. His defense was simple: they wanted a dating site, he built something bigger. Ideas, he argued, are cheap. Everyone has them.

**Block 22**
And the uncomfortable part is, he wasn't entirely wrong. Plenty of people dreamed of a campus network. Only one of them stayed up every night and actually shipped it.

**Block 23** *(viewer turn — channel spice)*
So here's the part that matters for you. You have probably had a brilliant idea in the shower. So has everyone alive. An idea sitting in your head is worth exactly nothing.

**Block 24** *(sign-off)*
The world doesn't reward the person who thinks of it. It rewards the person who builds it. So build yours, before someone else builds it for you. We'll be here with the next story.
